Level: College - ASL 101
State: Not applicable
Subject: American Sign Language
Resource type: Visual Practice Guide
Estimated time: 35-45 minutes
Standard / objective: Course objective - Apply basic visual-attention norms, fingerspell names, and produce
beginner greetings and classroom exchanges with appropriate nonmanual signals.
Source note: Course-level learning objective; no accreditation, transfer, or universal equivalency claimed. Static text descriptions
supplement, but do not replace, qualified live or video instruction.
What is included: branded cover, teacher guide, student directions, 16 original practice items, 5-item exit ticket,

Preparation: Print student pages single-sided or assign through a secure LMS. Review the key and select tools

rehearsal. Use each learner’s current plan; this guide does not replace it.

Exit Ticket
Complete independently in 8-10 minutes.
1. State the main skill or concept in your own words.
2. Create one accurate example that uses today’s skill.
3. Explain how you checked your example.
4. Identify one common error and correct it.
5. Rate your confidence from 1-4 and name your next practice step.
Quick rubric: 4 = accurate, complete, and well justified; 3 = accurate with minor omission; 2 = partial
understanding; 1 = limited evidence; 0 = blank or unrelated.

Complete Teacher Answer Key
1. Visual attention/eye contact.
2. Wave within sight, tap shoulder lightly, or use room light with permission.
3. ASL has its own grammar and nonmanual markers.
4. Names, places, and terms without a known sign.
5. Smoothly at readable pace, not letter-by-letter punching.
6. Raised eyebrows with appropriate body posture.
7. Furrowed/lowered eyebrows near the question.
8. Comfortable signing space visible to partner.
9. It is intrusive; use respectful visual/tactile norms.
10. A limited written notation for discussing signs, not ASL itself.
11. Attention, greeting, name exchange, closing.
12. Use an appropriate repeat/again request and puzzled facial expression.
13. It maintains attention and carries grammatical/social information.
14. Clear sightlines, adequate light, minimal visual obstruction.
15. Check hand position, rhythm, clarity, and eye gaze.
16. Short repeated practice with feedback from qualified sources.
Exit ticket guidance: Responses vary. Award full credit when the student accurately explains the focus, supplies
a correct example, describes a valid check, identifies and corrects a realistic misconception, and chooses a
specific next step.
Verification: Fixed responses and calculations were checked during production. Open responses are evaluated
against the criteria above.
